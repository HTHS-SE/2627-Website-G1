Astrophysics

Neutron Stars

Neutron stars begin their lifespan with an enormous bang! These stars are almost as dense as a black hole, and are the most dense material that we can directly observe (since the matter of black holes cannot be observed).

Neutron stars spin incredibly fast on their axis. They can spin many times a second.

✶ Pulsars

Pulsars are spinning neutron stars that emit light from their axes and high-energy cosmic rays. These stars are the main source of high-energy rays in the universe!

They are the strongest known magnets in the universe. This magnetic field accelerates the particles around it until they nearly reach the speed of light.

Quasars ✶

Similar to pulsars, quasars are bright nuclei powered by black holes. Though they are not neutron stars, they share many similarities. The strongest known quasars are thousands of times brighter than the entire milky way.







Dark Energy

The expansion of the universe is driven by dark energy - around 70% of the universe is dark energy. In the early 1900s, scientists realized that dark energy is present in the universe. They found that the speed at which other galaxies are moving away from us is increasing, because of this invisible energy! Dark energy is a very nebulous (no pun intended) concept. Is it a force that causes the universe to expand? Or something else?

“Vacuum energy” is a background energy that exists throughout the universe. This is a possible explanation for what scienists perceive as dark energy. However, this theory seems to contradict with the theories of the universe during calculations.

“Fluid energy” is theoretical fifth element that could be a combination of dark energy and dark matter.

Dark energy could be a defect in spacetime (like wrinkles in a fabric).

Dark energy could also be a contradiction to our current understanding of the universe, which should be modified with the more that we discover about our universe.







Black Holes

What Are They?

  • Celestial object with extremely high density - result in the warping of spacetime, creating strange effects
  • Gravitational pull is so strong that even light cannot escape, due also to its extreme density and curvature of spacetime
  • Created by the collapse of massive stars (much more massive than our sun)

Observed Behavior

  • Black holes are invisible to telescopes - they emit no light, since not even light can escape a black hole
  • Observed by their gravitational effect on other objects nearby
  • Gaia BH1 is the closest black hole to earth & TON 618 is the most massive black hole known at 6.6 billion times the mass of the sun

Inside the Black Hole

  • Once the event horizon is passed, nothing can escape
  • What happens inside of a black hole is currently unknown, but there is the process of “spagettification”, where an object becomes stretched out extremely thin.
  • Upon entry, space and time becomes distorted for the viewer due to the immense gravitational pull
