Astronomy

Astronomy is the study of planets, stars, galaxies, and the entirety of the universe! We start from the beginning of time, when the Big Bang occurred approximately 13.8 billion years ago, to the very spot that NASA’s telescopes may have been looking at mere seconds ago. Astronomy itself is an observational science.

Astronomy, though studied through gamma rays, radiation, and other remnants of celestial bodies, has a wide variety of subdivisions beneath it. This includes sectors like astrometry, the mapping of celestial bodies; astrobiology, and the exploration for life that may exist in space; astrophysics, how the laws of physics can be applied to study distant objects; and astrogeology, what the physical materials in space are composed of.

There are two types of astronomy: theoretical and observational. In observational astronomy, data is taken straight from space. In theoretical astronomy, machine learning models and other algorithms are created to predict the outcome of parts of astronomy.

Astrography: The Study of Stars

Specifically, astrography is mapping or photography of celestial bodies and their orbits. It includes their coordinates and any methods used to map them, which has changed many times over the years. Also referred to as uranography, it includes any naming and referral to these planets, galaxies, or stars.

This article on astrography is going to focus entirely on stars, including how they make energy and form constellations. Though it is more a topic of astrophysics, this section also includes the lifecycles of stars, including how they can go supernova and explode. After stars die, their light can still be seen for thousands of years afterwards, meaning we keep using them as a guide for years on end.

Astrobiology: Life in the Cosmos

Astrobiology is the study of life in space! Scientists use precise mathematical algorithms and space experiments to locate the origin of where life may be in other galaxies or bodies in our own solar system. This has been supported by expeditions by the Europa Clipper; Mars 2020: Perseverance; OSIRIS-REx; and the Habitable Worlds Observatory.

Here, you can learn about those expeditions, the intricacies of space, and the six main focus areas of astrobiology: prebiotic chemistry, co-evolution, habitable worlds, search for life, celestial navigation, and tech innovations!

Astrophysics: Science & Celestial Bodies

Astrophysics is the application of physical sciences to celestial bodies. Scientists apply the laws of physics, such as gravity, nuclear physics, thermodynamics, and even quantum physics to analyze the behavior of various objects. Researchers in this field look at objects like dark matter, exoplanets, and much more.

In this website, you’ll learn about different types of objects observed in the universe, such as dark energy, black holes, and neutron stars. Read along to see the types of phenomena seen in space and find out how they work.
