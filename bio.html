A History of Life In Space

As Told by Astrobiology Research

4.6 Billion Years Ago

The Sun, a swirling cloud of gas and dust, contained ingredients such as carbon, water, and other organic building blocks of life. These parts came from the remnants of old generations of stars that had exploded and scattered their parts.

4.5 Billion Years Ago

The Earth forms from a disk of gas that circles the Sun, containing dust. This disk continues to rotate, and rotate, and rotate, until it collides with a body that forms the moon. Shortly after, the surface of this disk cools rapidly and creates a crust, oceans, and lava basins.

3.7 Billion Years Ago

The earliest lifeforms begin to appear during the Archean Era 3.7 billion years ago. Oceans begin to fill with iron ions that lead to the coming of life forms. These do not rely on oxygen just yet.

3.1 Billion Years Ago

Life finally begins evolving and coming to be in full force. Abiogenesis causes the sparking of life on Earth due to the liquid-water environment. There is finally official, organic life in the solar system.

Present Day

Through the period of 3.1 Billion years ago and our present day, life continues to evolve at an immense rate. Humans emerge some hundred thousand years ago, and now, scientists study the very thing that aided them in coming to be. Astrobiology becomes a highly sought after topic in astronomy.

Coming Research

Now, astrobiology research has gained traction for its 6 main fields: prebiotic chemistry and the origin of life; co-evolution of life and the Earth; formation and identification of habitable worlds; search for life beyond Earth; Artemis and the moon to Mars; and technological developments.


Humans' Attempts at Reaching Life Beyond

We've always been excited to see our fellow lifeforms. So we sent out missions.

Europa Clipper

October 14, 2024

Europa Clipper was Earth's first mission where a rover was sent out to withstand the conditions of Jupiter's moon, Europa, and see if there was a salty ocean beneath the icy surface that could sustain life.

Mars 2020: Perseverance

February 18, 2021

Mars 2020: Perseverance, alongside the aerial bot Ingenuity, went out into space to study the geology of Mars and seek ancient microbial life, and return samples to Earth. These samples would then be used for future research.

OSIRIS-REx

September 8, 2016

The OSIRIS-REx (Origins, Spectral Interpretation, Resource Identification, Security-Regolith Explorer) is a breakthrough study that observed asteroids to see if they contained organic materials and nucleobases to study the origin of life on Earth.

Habitable Worlds Observatory

Still In-Design

The Habitable Worlds Observatory, or HWO, is a proposed flagship telescope that could potentially look for habitable, Earth-sized exoplanets in habitable zones. Its goal is to bring about the possibility of human space migration.
